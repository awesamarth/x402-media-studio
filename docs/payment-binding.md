# Payment, quote ownership and replay boundary

The documented exact EVM authorization signs transfer fields (payer, recipient, amount, validity and nonce), not our quote ID or HTTP body. Our earlier description overstated this as a demonstrated cross-customer theft attack. A payer choosing another own equal-priced quote is order interchangeability. Cross-customer misuse additionally requires a stolen unused payment authorization or inducing its owner to approve funding a different purchase. An unsigned resource field does not provide signed order intent, and we make no such guarantee.

The implementation follows ordinary x402 authorization semantics without requiring unsupported custom signing. Ordinary authenticated calls create immutable quotes under genuine configured server-side account principals. In paid mode, the public purchase accepts only quote ID and idempotency key; it resolves owner, workflow and price from that persisted quote. Any payer whose authorization is verified by the trusted facilitator may fund it. This does not change the quote owner or grant access to that owner's quote, job or artifacts. The public response is a minimal purchase acknowledgment; the owner uses authenticated get_purchase to retrieve the eventual job ID. No wallet enrollment or additional consent workflow has been introduced.

API_ACCOUNTS_JSON maps stable account IDs to bearer tokens configured by the operator. No accounts or credentials are generated automatically. LOCAL_DEV_TOKEN remains a single-account development fallback with principal local-owner. Data routes enforce account ownership; asset access is granted only after that account uploads the bytes. Token rotation can preserve the account ID. This is a small static-account deployment design, not a complete self-service identity product.

ACCOUNT_PAYER was a temporary fixture constraint and has been removed. Verified payer evidence is dynamic and checked consistently between the authorization, verify response and settlement response. Facilitator choice and compatibility still require verification before enabling payments. BILLING_MODE defaults to free; no live settings have been configured.

Before settlement, the service durably reserves payment identity (network, asset, payer, nonce) and binds it to the quote/purchase key. The purchase and enqueue locks serialize concurrent calls within the supported single process. A quote cannot be purchased under a changed key; an already-reserved authorization cannot fund another quote. Successful purchase retries return the same acknowledgment and job without settling again. An ambiguous settlement remains pending for operator reconciliation without another charge. Quote, payment, receipt and job records must be retained consistently. Multi-process use is unsupported.

Unused payment authorization headers are credentials: TLS, secret redaction and avoiding logs are required. A stolen unused authorization can still be spent for its permitted transfer; first-observed nonce binding does not prove first-use quote intent. The service does not solve that theft or claim a signed-order guarantee. No payment header, bearer token or authorization payload is returned in ordinary output or stored in the purchase ledger. Protocol PAYMENT-RESPONSE contains public settlement evidence, as required by x402.

References: [exact EVM scheme](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/schemes/exact/scheme_exact_evm.md), [x402 v2 specification](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/x402-specification-v2.md). No PayBox SIWX, custom HTTP-signature or signed quote extension support is assumed.

Remaining genuine decisions: receiving address, facilitator/service authentication contract, public hostname and deployment approval. Static customer accounts must be configured through the operator's chosen credential process. Self-service account onboarding can be designed later; it is not needed to test the current configurable boundary. Model/language choices are separate from payment.

Free mode uses authenticated POST /workflows with immutable ownership and idempotency. It never invokes the facilitator. Paid-mode errors do not fall back to free execution.
